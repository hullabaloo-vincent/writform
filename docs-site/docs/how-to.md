# How-to

## Write a document with others

1. Open **Documents** → **New document**, name it, and pick a format (or
   leave it as Document).
2. Type. Everyone with write access sees your changes and cursor live.
3. Select text and click the comment button to leave feedback anchored to
   it. Toggle the feedback panel with the speech-bubble icon.
4. **Share** (owner only) to grant a friend or a whole group read or write
   access. Group shares post a card in that group's chat.
5. **Version history** shows autosaved snapshots and any you name
   yourself. Preview or restore any of them.

## Set up the page and print

1. In a **Document**, open the **Layout** tab of the ribbon for margins,
   paper, orientation and columns, or the sliders button for everything:
   gutter, facing pages, headers and footers, page numbers. **Margins ▸
   Custom margins…** opens that panel at Margins: type a figure (1.1, say)
   or step by quarter inches with − and +.
2. For a header or footer, type in its left, centre or right slot and use
   **Add a field** for the page number, page count, title, author or date.
   Tick *Different first page* to leave a title page clean.
3. Press the eye button to **preview** every page as it will print.
4. **⌘P** (Ctrl+P) prints. **Export** saves the same pages as a PDF, or the
   document as Word.

To make the page larger or smaller while you write, click the percentage
beside the word count, or hold Ctrl (⌘ on a Mac) and turn the wheel.
**Page width** and **Whole page** fit it to the window.

A new Document starts on the paper of where you are (US Letter or A4).
Change it with the paper list on the **Layout** tab and the document keeps
your choice for everyone.

To write in another language, open the sliders button, go to **Details**
and set the **language**. Spelling is then checked in that language and
quote marks are typed in its style.

## Export a PDF

**Export ▸ Export PDF…** opens the choices. They are remembered on this
device.

1. **Pictures.** *Screen* keeps the file small enough to send. *Print*
   keeps pictures sharp on paper. *Original* puts each picture in as the
   file it is, which can make a large PDF.
2. **Tags for screen readers** writes headings, lists, tables and picture
   descriptions into the file, so it can be read aloud. Give each picture
   a description first (select it, then **Picture ▸ Description**). The
   export says how many have none.
3. **Archive copy (PDF/A)** makes a file meant to open the same way in
   many years. Choose it for a file you will keep or hand to an archive.
4. **Keep an editable copy inside** puts the document itself into the PDF.
   Import that PDF later and you get the document back with its styles
   and pictures. Anyone you send the PDF to can do the same, so leave it
   off for a file that is only meant to be read.
5. **Details** holds the subject, the keywords and the language. They are
   kept with the document.
6. **Create PDF**, then **Save…**.

To get a document back out of its PDF, use Documents → **Import** and pick
the PDF. If it holds its document you are asked whether to open the
document or to read the pages as text.

For a book, **Export ▸ Paperback PDF (print-ready)…** has two choices of
its own for a printer that asks for them: **PDF/X-1a for a printer** and
**With bleed**.

## Format a document

Anything the ribbon does can also be asked for by name. Press **⌘K**
(Ctrl+K), type a word or two of what you want ("checklist", "margins",
"zoom page width") and press Enter. **⌘/** lists every key.

1. Select words: a small menu appears above them with bold, italic,
   underline, colour, highlight, link and comment. The ribbon's **Home**
   tab has those and the font, size and style. The **⋯** button beside
   them has the rest (strikethrough, sub- and superscript, change case,
   clear formatting). With nothing selected, a choice applies to what you
   type next.
2. For a whole paragraph: alignment, line spacing and the indent buttons
   are in the ribbon, and shading and borders are under the **⋯** beside
   them. Exact indents (a hanging indent, a first line),
   *exactly*/*at least* line heights and *keep with next* are in the
   inspector's **Text** tab (the sliders button).
3. Use **styles** for anything you'll do twice. Pick Title, Heading 1 or
   Caption from the styles button. To change how every heading looks, open
   the styles button and choose **Modify Heading 1…**, or format one
   heading by hand and choose **Update Heading 1 to match selection**.
   **New style from selection…** saves a look under a name of your own.
4. To copy formatting from one place to another: put the caret in the
   text that has it, choose **⋯ ▸ Copy formatting to other text**, then
   select the text to give it to.
5. ⌘⇧9 (or the checklist button) starts a **checklist**. Click a box to
   tick it.

## Let the app fix slips as you type

1. Open **Settings ▸ Writing** and tick **Correct as I type**. It is on
   to begin with on a computer.
2. Untick any kind of correction you do not want. Each has its own line.
3. **Your corrections…** shows every word that is changed and what it
   becomes. Add your own (a shortening you always type, say), remove any
   you disagree with, and list words to leave exactly as typed.
4. When it changes something you meant, press **Backspace** straight
   away, or Undo once. The correction is taken back and your typing stays.

## Find your way around a long document

- **Go to a page or a heading:** click the page count beside the word
  count. Type `12`, `+2`, or a few words of a heading, and press Enter.
- **Go back to where you were writing:** press **Shift+F5**. Press it
  again for the place before.
- **Move a section:** open the outline, press **Reorder**, and use the
  arrows beside a heading. Up and down move it past its neighbours. Left
  and right change its level.
- **Work on one chapter at a time:** open the outline and press the
  button at the end of the chapter's row. The bar over the page steps to
  the chapters either side. **Show everything** ends it.
- **See why text looks the way it does:** open the sliders panel. The top
  of its Text tab names the style, then what the paragraph and the words
  have of their own, with a Clear beside each.

## Add a table, a footnote or a table of contents

1. **A table**: Insert ▸ Table and pick a size. Tab moves from cell to cell.
   With the caret in the table, the **Table** tab has rows, columns, merge,
   header row, lines and shading. A long row is cut across pages where the
   page ends.
2. **A footnote**: put the caret where the reference goes and choose
   Insert ▸ Footnote (or Endnote). Write the note in the box that opens,
   then press Enter to go back to the text. Click a note's number to read
   or change it, or scroll to the end of the document, where all its
   footnotes and endnotes are listed and can be changed in place.
3. **A table of contents**: Insert ▸ Contents, usually near the top. It
   lists Heading 1–3 and updates as you write. Page numbers show in the
   preview, in print and in the PDF.
4. **A landscape page, or a part in two columns**: put the caret in the
   paragraph where it should start, choose Layout ▸ Section ▸ *Start a new
   section*, then set that section's orientation or columns. Start another
   section where it should end.

## Suggest changes instead of making them

1. On the ribbon's **Review** tab, set the mode to **Suggesting** (in the
   other formats: open Feedback and choose **Changes**, where the same
   switch is). A *Suggesting* tag stays on the ribbon while it is on.
2. Edit as usual. What you add is underlined in your colour, what you
   delete is struck through and stays in place, and a paragraph you split
   or join shows a ¶ where the break is. Formatting (bold, styles, lists)
   still changes directly.
3. To decide: put the caret in a change and press **Accept** or **Reject**
   on the Review tab (they move on to the next change), or open
   **Changes** for the whole list (who, when and what) with the same two
   buttons on each, *Accept all* and *Reject all*, and a button to comment
   on a change.
4. **Viewing** shows the text as it would read with everything accepted and
   locks it. Switch back to Editing or Suggesting to go on.

The mode is your own: someone else can be editing the same document while
you suggest. It is remembered for that document on that device, so you
don't start editing by accident next time. Printing, PDFs and the word
count use the text with the suggestions accepted. *Export Word* carries
them as Word's tracked changes, and *Export Word (clean copy)* leaves them
out. Taking back your own suggestion (Backspace over what you just typed)
simply removes it.

## Listen to a document

Put the caret where to start and choose **Read aloud** (on the Review tab,
or the speaker button in the header for the other formats). The sentence
being read is highlighted. The bar that opens has pause, back and on a
sentence, stop, speed and (where your system has more than one) a voice.
It uses the voices installed on your device.

## Check a document before you send it

Choose **Check** on the Review tab, the check button in the header, or
press **F7**. The panel reads the whole text and lists what it found
under headings such as Punctuation, Left in the text and Tidy up, with a
count beside each.

Open a finding to see every place it occurs. Click a place to select it in
the text. **Fix** changes that place. **Fix all** changes every place at
once and one undo brings them all back. **Skip** sets a place aside. It
stays aside for you and your co-writers until the words there change.
**Set aside** does the same for a whole finding: just for now, in this
document, or in all your documents.

Some findings are a thing done two ways, such as straight and curly quote
marks. The panel shows how often each is used and takes the commoner one
to be meant. Choose the other if that is the one you want. The places
written the other way are then the ones listed.

If you are in **Suggesting** mode, each fix is made as a suggestion that
you or your co-writer can accept or reject. In **Viewing** mode and in a
document you can only read, the findings are listed without Fix.

## See how a draft reads

Open **Check** and choose the **Reports** tab. It is there in a
manuscript and in a Document.

Start with **Chapter by chapter** in a book. Each row is a chapter. A
number is underlined where that chapter stands well apart from the rest:
far longer, almost no speech, sentences much longer than usual. Choose a
row and you are taken to that chapter, and every other report is now
about it. **Whole book** at the top takes you back.

**Sentences** draws every sentence as a bar. A flat stretch is a run of
sentences of one length. Click a bar to go to its sentence. Below the
chart are the patterns it found, such as four sentences in a row that
begin with "She". Choose one and step through its places with the arrows
at the foot of the panel.

**Repeats** lists words that come again within a few lines, and phrases
you lean on. If a word is meant, open the menu beside it and choose
**This is fine**. **Watch this word** does the opposite: the word is
pointed out every time, in the Checks tab.

**Word choice** and **Dialogue** read English. Each lists what it found
by kind and then by word. Where a row has **Fix**, the change is plain
enough to make for you: "nodded his head" becomes "nodded", and "Go."
she said becomes "Go," she said. In a novel the Word choice report leaves
speech alone. Tick **Look inside quote marks too** to include it.

**Pace** draws the book as a band. Tall blue bars are stretches with
almost no speech and long sentences or paragraphs. A long run of them is
listed below with what was measured, such as "looks back 14 times". Click
the band to go to that stretch. A quiet stretch can be exactly right:
this only shows you where they are.

To look at one passage only, select it in the text and choose
**Selection**.

## Keep a house style

Open **Check** and choose the **Rules** tab. The switch at the top picks
whose rules you are looking at.

- **This document**: rules kept with the document. Everyone who writes it
  sees the same ones, and a copy of the document keeps them.
- **All my documents**: your own rules, kept on this device and used in
  every document you open. You can also reach them from Settings ▸
  Writing ▸ Your rules for Check.

**Spell in** chooses the English the text is held to: American, British,
British with -ize, Canadian or Australian. With none chosen the text is
only held to whichever it mostly uses, and the panel asks you which you
meant when it finds both.

In either place you can untick a check you do not want, and add words:

- **Write one for another**: type what to find and what to write. Each
  place it is found gets a Fix.
- **Words to look at**: a word you want pointed out, with a note to
  yourself if you like. Nothing is changed for you.
- **Never flag**: a word or phrase no check should raise.

In a shared document, tick **Use only this document's rules** when the
whole team should see one list. **Copy my rules into this document** puts
your own words into the document's rules for everyone.

**Save to a file** writes the rules you are looking at to a file. **Add
from a file** on another device, or in another document, brings them in.

## Compare two versions

- **With an earlier version:** History, open a version, **Compare with
  now**.
- **With a Word file:** Review tab ▸ **Compare**, and pick the `.docx`.

The two are shown side by side with the paragraphs lined up: what was taken
out is marked on the left and what was put in on the right. The arrows step
through the differences. *Whole text* shows the unchanged paragraphs too,
*One column* puts both versions in one, and the swap button turns the
sides round. Comparing doesn't change anything.

Older versions of the app can't open a document once it uses colour,
highlight, a font or size on part of a paragraph, indents, borders,
checklists, tables, footnotes, a table of contents, sections or suggested
changes. They're asked to update, and nothing is lost. If your server
hasn't been updated yet those controls don't appear for its documents, so
update the server first (see [Setup](setup.md#updating-a-server)).

## See the printed pages while you write

Press the page-view button (the split sheet in the header). The sheet is
divided into the pages the PDF will have: the same text on each, the same
count, with the break falling inside a paragraph or between the rows of a
table when that is where the page turns. It keeps up as you type. The gaps
between the pages stay where they are and the text moves across them. The
lines themselves are the screen's. For the page exactly
as printed, line for line, open **Preview** (the eye). If page view
misbehaves on your device, Settings ▸ Writing ▸ *Page view shows the pages
as they print* switches to the simpler view.

## Give a book its cover

Open a manuscript, press the book button and choose **Cover**.

1. **Pictures.** Choose a JPEG or a PNG for the front cover. It is the
   ebook's cover and the front of the paperback. A tall picture of
   1,600 × 2,560 pixels suits the ebook stores. For the paperback, add a
   back and a spine too, or one picture of the whole cover. A printed
   cover is one sheet with the back, the spine and the front side by
   side, and the tab says what that sheet measures for your trim size,
   paper and page count.
2. **Open the cover editor.** The sheet is drawn as it prints. Use the
   tabs at the top to look at the whole cover, the back, the spine or the
   front. *Guides* shows what the printer trims off, the margin to keep
   words out of, where the spine folds and where the barcode goes.
3. **Add words.** *Start with the book's title and author* puts the
   title, the author, a spine line and the blurb in their usual places.
   Click any of them to change what it says, its font, size and colour.
   Drag it where it belongs and pull its side handles to set its width.
4. **Place a picture.** Click a part of the cover that has no words on
   it. You can choose its picture or its paper colour there, make the
   picture larger, and drag it to choose which part shows.
5. **Save.** *Make the cover* builds the PDF to upload to the printer.
   *Make a template* builds a PDF at the exact size with the guides on
   it, for artwork you make in a drawing program.

Finish the text before the cover. The spine's width depends on the page
count, and the editor warns you when it has changed since the artwork
went in. Books under 80 pages have a spine too narrow for words. The
sizes are Amazon KDP's. Another printer may ask for different ones, so
check their template.

Every font the editor offers is open-licensed and free to use on a book
you sell.

## Show a book's cover and blurb in your lists

A book's card in Documents shows its cover, its description and its tags
to you and to everyone it is shared with.

1. Open the manuscript, press the book button and choose **Book**.
2. Write the **Description**. It is the blurb on the card and in the
   ebook's details.
3. Add **Tags** with commas between them, such as *Vampires, Dark
   romance*. Up to twelve show on the card.
4. Give the book a front cover on the **Cover** tab. The card shows the
   front as you designed it a moment after you change it.

A Document's card shows its subject and keywords, set under **Details**
in its settings.

## Give a book's chapters their look

Open a manuscript, press the book button and choose **Design**.

- **Your own scene break.** Under **Scene breaks**, choose **Your own
  picture** and a JPEG or a PNG (blood drops, a pair of fangs). A
  picture on white or on a see-through ground works best. **Height**
  sets how tall it prints. For characters of your own instead, choose
  **Ornament**, then **Your own…**, and type up to 12.
- **Art behind each chapter's first page.** Under **Chapter art**, choose
  a picture for **Behind the first page**. The chapter's heading and its
  first lines are printed over it. Set **Words on a picture** to **Light**
  for dark art.
- **A picture facing each chapter.** Choose one for **Facing the first
  page**. It fills the left-hand page and the chapter opens on the right.
- **One chapter different.** Under the pictures, **Give a chapter its
  own** and pick the chapter. Set its **Behind** or **Facing** to its own
  picture or to none. Only the chapters that differ are listed. The ×
  beside one gives it the book's pictures again.
- **What goes under each chapter's title.** Under **Chapter openings**,
  **Under the title** takes a typed ornament, **Your own…** characters, or
  **Same as the scene breaks**, which follows the scene breaks' ornament
  or picture as you change it.

A whole page wants a picture of 300 pixels an inch: for a 6 × 9 in book,
2,775 pixels tall. When you export the paperback for a printer, tick
**With bleed** so the art runs to the edge of the paper.

## Place and frame a picture

Click a picture to select it. The **Picture** tab opens.

- **Move it.** Drag the handle on its top left corner to where it should
  go. A line shows the place between two blocks. *Up* and *Down* on the
  tab, or ⌥↑ and ⌥↓, move it one block at a time.
- **Set it left, centre or right** with the Position buttons.
- **Frame it.** *Border* picks a colour, and the box beside it the
  thickness. *Corners* rounds them, all the way to a circle.
- **Size it** by dragging a corner, or type inches under *Size*.
- **Caption** puts a line of text under it. **Description** says what it
  shows for someone who can't see it.

## Bring in a Word document

Documents → **Import**, pick the `.docx`. Headings and styles, lists and
checklists, tables, footnotes, a table of contents, links, pictures, fonts,
colours, spacing, indents, sections and the page setup come across, and
Word's tracked changes arrive as suggested changes you can accept or reject
(see above). A note after the import says what couldn't be kept (text
boxes, equations, drawn shapes). A table laid out wider than its page is
made to fit, and a cell's own borders and a picture's outline and rounded
corners are kept. A file that looks like a book comes in as a Manuscript
with its chapters found.

## Bring in a PDF

Documents → **Import**, pick the PDF. What comes back is a document you can
edit, not a picture of the pages.

- **Text** comes back as paragraphs, headings, lists and tables, with
  links, footnotes and pictures where they stood. Two columns are read
  down one and then down the other.
- **The page** comes with it: paper, margins, columns, the font and size
  of the text, and the running head and page numbers as the document's
  header and footer.
- **A password** is asked for if the file has one.
- **A scan** holds pictures of pages and no text. You are asked whether to
  **Read the text** or **Keep as pictures**. Reading is done on this
  device by the system's own recogniser (Mac, iPhone and iPad), a page at
  a time, and is worth a read through afterwards.
- **A long file** shows how far it is. **Cancel** stops it and nothing is
  made.

A note after the import says what was left out. A PDF made by a layout
program for print will come back less tidy than one made from a word
processor, and a PDF with tags (Word and Google Docs write them when asked
to) comes back best of all.

## Edit a PDF

Documents → **Import**, pick the PDF, and choose **Edit this PDF**. (The
other choice, **Convert to a document**, makes a document of what it says.
See above.)

- **Read and find.** Scroll the pages, zoom with the two magnifier buttons,
  and type in **Find in this PDF**. Enter goes to the next match.
- **Pages.** The list on the left is the pages. Click one to pick it. Shift
  and Ctrl or ⌘ pick several. Drag to move them, or use the buttons above
  the list to turn, move, copy, add and take out pages. *Save these pages
  as a PDF of their own* splits them out.
- **Mark up.** Select text on a page and press **Highlight**, or the
  underline or strike through buttons beside it. **Note**, **Draw**,
  **Box**, **Arrow** and **Type** each make their mark where you click or
  drag. Back in **Read**, click a mark to move it, change its words or take
  it off.
- **Fill a form.** Its fields are shaded on the page. Type in them and tick
  them there.
- **Sign.** Press **Sign**, draw your signature (or use a picture of it),
  then click the page where it goes. *Make part of the page* fixes
  signatures and typed words in place so they can no longer be moved.
- **Change text (Beta).** Click a piece of the page's own text and retype
  it. A PDF holds only the letters it uses, so a letter it lacks is set in
  a standard font and you are told. Check the result.
- **Undo** and **Redo** are in the header, and ⌘Z works.
- **Save.** *Save a copy* writes the PDF as it now is to a file you
  choose. Your original is not touched. In the apps, *Keep on this device*
  puts it in Documents so you can come back to it, and **Save** then saves
  it in place.

A PDF with a password asks for it. If whoever made it does not allow
changes, the tools for those changes are off. If it is signed with a
certificate you are warned first, since any change breaks that signature.

## Keep what's on this device in order

- **Folders**: Documents → the folder-plus button → name it and choose *On
  this device* (with no server connected there is nothing to choose). Open
  a document's ⋯ menu → **Move to folder…** to put it in one, or select
  several and press **Move**. Deleting a folder never deletes what's in it.
- **Search**: type in the search field. Documents on this device are found
  by any word in them, with the passage shown on the card. The same field
  in **Canvas** finds boards by name and by the words on them.
- **Publish**: a document's or board's ⋯ menu → **Publish to a server…**
  puts a copy on the server you're connected to (a board asks which group).
  Not connected? It opens the connect sheet, and carries on once you are.
  The original stays on this device and the two don't sync afterwards.
- **Your picture**: Settings → Profile → **Choose a picture**. With no
  server connected this is the profile on this device. When connected, use
  *Apply to this server* to copy it there.

## Run a writing session

1. Open **Sessions**, pick the channel, **+ New session**, name it.
2. **+ Add a prompt**: write it with the formatting toolbar, choose a
   **Timer** (or **Another length** for your own number of minutes),
   **Add prompt**.
3. Press **Start** when everyone's ready. Members write on their own private
   page, and the side chat stays open the whole time.
4. To write with nothing else on screen, press the arrows button at the end
   of the bar above your page. Escape brings the session back.
5. **Stop** early if you like, or let the timer end the prompt. Everyone's
   writing is then shown. Add more prompts or **End session**.
6. To keep what you wrote, press **Save as a document** on your piece. It
   becomes a Document of your own. **Copy** takes any piece as plain text.
7. To pick a winner, everyone presses **Vote** on the piece they liked
   best. Press it again to take the vote back. When everyone has voted,
   whoever started the prompt presses **Close voting**, and each piece
   shows how many votes it got.

## Invite people to a group

Group name header → **Invite** (admins only) → share the temporary code.
They redeem it from the **+** button in the chat sidebar.

## Add a custom emote

Composer → smiley button → **+ Add emote** (admins only) → upload an image
and name it. Everyone can then type `:name:`.

## Join voice

Click a channel under **VOICE** in the chat sidebar and grant microphone
access. Mute and leave from the pill in the status bar, which stays with
you in every app.

## Turn on your camera or share your screen

While in a voice channel, use the camera button in the status-bar pill
(grant camera access the first time). The video panel opens when someone's
video appears. Drag it anywhere, resize from the corner, and it stays up
while you work in other apps. The screen-share button appears on platforms
that support capture (Windows/Linux). Everyone, including macOS, can watch.

## Share a note

Notes → open the note → **Share** → pick a friend. They get a card in your
DM with a **Save to vault** button.

## Use the canvas

- Click tools in the bottom bar to place stickies / text / frames.
- **Paste** an image or a URL directly onto the board.
- Shift-click (or shift-drag on empty space) to select several elements.
- Select a frame to change its color. Select a sticky or text element for
  formatting (bold/italic/underline, size, alignment, bullets).
- Select a connector to change its anchors, dash style, and end decorations.
- Toggle **snap to grid** in the toolbar for evenly aligned elements.
- From a document, use **Send to canvas** to place a live excerpt card on
  one of the group's boards.

## Force-logout a device

Settings → **Devices** shows every login for your account, and you can
revoke any of them. Server admins can force-logout any user from the
**Admin** tab.

## Choose who can make an account on your server

A new server takes its first account (yours, as its admin) and then anyone
who can reach it. To change that, open Settings → **Admin** → **New
accounts** and choose:

- **Anyone who can reach this server**, as at first.
- **Only someone with an invite code.** Set how many accounts a code may
  make and for how many days, then **Make an invite code**. It is copied
  for you to send. Revoke one there at any time.
- **No one.**

People see the choice on the sign-in form: no **Create account** tab when
the server takes no one, and a field for the code when it asks for one.

## Reset a forgotten password

There's no email on a self-hosted server, so recovery goes through your
server admin:

1. Ask a server admin to open Settings → **Admin** and generate a one-time
   reset code for your account.
2. On the server's sign-in form, choose **Forgot password**, enter your username,
   the code, and a new password.
3. This revokes every existing session for your account, so you'll need to
   log back in everywhere.
